# Octos's voice

How Octos talks to Farhat's customers on WhatsApp, and the real
conversations that teach it. Everything Octos says, whether a template,
a free reply or a handover to staff, is held up against what is in here.

## The bar

It should read like what happens at the counter every day, not like
software. If a reply would sound wrong in `kojo-week.md`, it is wrong.

## What lives here

- `kojo-week.md`: the reference conversation. One customer, one week:
  free replies, a staff handover, one template, the 24-hour window.
- `real/`: real exchanges collected from the branch phones, one file per
  conversation. These become the voice guide and the test library.
- `voice-guide.md` (later): the rules, written from `real/`, not
  invented.

## Collecting a real conversation

One file per conversation in `real/`, numbered:
`real/001-job-ready-evening.md`, `real/002-stickers-price.md`.

    ---
    branch: Westland
    when: evening, after closing
    topic: job status
    ---
    Customer: Good evening boss please is my job ready
    Staff: Yes ready, come for it tomorrow
    Customer: Ok thank you
    ---
    Octos should: find the job by the customer's number, say ready or
    not with the promised time, give the collection code if ready.

The "Octos should" line is what turns a conversation into a test.

## Privacy, before anything is saved

- No phone numbers, ever.
- Change every name, customer and staff. Keep the way they write:
  "boss", "please", "chale", "oo", spelling as typed.
- No job codes, receipt numbers or payment references.
- Describe photos and files in brackets, such as `[sends logo]` or
  `[sends 2 PDFs]`. Never save the actual artwork.
- Prices and quantities can stay; they are not personal.

## What makes a good example

The ordinary ones matter most: "please are you around", "is my job
ready", "do you do X", a file with "5 copies". Then the hard ones: the
angry customer, the vague one, the one who sends a voice note, the one
who changes their mind. Include how staff actually replied, including
replies that went badly; those teach the most.
